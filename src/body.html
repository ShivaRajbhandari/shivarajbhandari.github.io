Honors thesis · 2026

★ Best Thesis Award · UNC Department of Public Policy

Movement-Mediated Vertical Policy Feedback

How can local governments advance climate policy at higher levels? Drawing on 41 interviews with organizers and officials in Austin, Charlotte and Portland, I argue that cities do it, often without meaning to, by resourcing and channeling social movements.

Advisors: Dr. Angel Hsu and Dr. Elizabeth Korver-Glenn

Why did Nepal’s Discord-organized Gen Z uprising produce lasting electoral change when peer movements collapsed? Built on interviews with 23 activists and five politicians during an independent study in Kathmandu, supported by the Morehead-Cain Lovelace Fund for Discovery.

A course I designed and taught · Spring 2026

Resistance to Authoritarianism

Through UNC’s Carolina Student Training and Research program (C-START), I taught my peers a one-credit course I built from my own research. After trips to Chile, Uruguay, Peru and Mexico, I designed a semester of weekly two-hour seminars on how movements have resisted and outlasted authoritarian rule, using primary sources, documentary film and guest speakers, and asked what they can teach us about defending democratic institutions.

Mentors: Professor Gabrielle Calvocoressi and Dr. Evelyne Huber
